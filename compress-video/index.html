Why the video will not send

An email bounces at 25 MB. Discord stops at 10 MB without Nitro. A form somewhere says the file is too large, and the file is a forty-second clip off a phone.

Phones record generously. A minute of 1080p video straight off a camera can run a couple of hundred megabytes, because the phone's priority is quality, not your email provider's attachment cap.

Compression re-encodes the video at a lower bitrate, and this tool works backwards from the number you actually care about: the size limit. Tell it the video has to be under 25 MB and it computes the bitrate that fits, splits it sensibly between the picture and the sound, and tells you honestly what that bitrate will look like before you spend time encoding.

Sometimes the honest answer is that the target is too tight at full resolution. When that happens the tool says so, and offers the resolution that fixes it. A 720p video that looks clean beats a 1080p video made of smudges.

What the quality estimate means

The meter is arithmetic, not magic: the bitrate divided by the pixels it has to cover each second. It is a good predictor, and it is still an estimate. Fast-moving footage is harder to compress than a still interview, and no formula knows which one you dropped.

That is what the Preview button is for. It encodes a few one-second samples of your actual video at the exact settings the full encode would use, and shows them next to the original. What you see is what you will get, because it is the same encoder doing the same work. If the encoder still overshoots your target, the tool runs one corrected second pass automatically and keeps the smaller result.

How this stays private

The tool is built on mediabunny, an open-source media engine, and your browser's own built-in video encoder. Dropping your first video pulls the engine, about 660 KB, from this site. From then on everything runs on your device: the video is read from disk into the tab, re-encoded there, and handed back as a download.

Your video is never uploaded. There is no server that could receive it. You can verify that: open your browser's DevTools Network tab and compress something. You will see the engine come down from this domain once, and nothing go up, ever.

The offline question deserves a precise answer rather than a generous one. Before your first video, the engine has not arrived, so the tool needs the network once. After it has arrived, you can disconnect and keep compressing.

The full source is public on GitHub under the AGPL-3.0 license, and the privacy page lists this tool's one download and the moment it happens.

Questions

How do I compress a video to 10 MB for Discord?

Drop the video, press the 10 MB preset, and compress. The tool computes the bitrate that lands under 10 MB and tells you what quality to expect first.

If ten megabytes is too tight for the length of your clip at full resolution, it will say so and suggest the resolution that works.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. It is read into the browser tab and re-encoded there by your device's own hardware.

Watch the DevTools Network tab while it runs: a 660 KB engine comes down from this site on your first file, and nothing goes up on any of them.

Will there be a watermark?

No watermark, no account, no daily limit, no paid tier that removes any of those.

The output is your video, smaller, and nothing else.

Why does it say my target is unreachable?

The audio track is copied through untouched, so it has a fixed cost. If your target is smaller than the audio plus a minimum workable video bitrate, no encoder settings can hit it honestly.

The message tells you the smallest target that can work for that clip at that resolution. A shorter clip, a larger target, a lower resolution, or a lower frame rate are the ways out.

What formats can I drop, and what comes out?

MP4, MOV, WebM, and MKV go in, subject to what your browser can decode. An MP4 with H.264 video comes out, which is the most widely accepted combination there is.

When the audio codec fits MP4, which covers nearly everything phones and browsers produce, the track is copied as it is, not re-encoded, so it loses nothing.

Why doesn't it work in my browser?

The tool needs the WebCodecs API, which is how a web page reaches your device's hardware video encoder. Chrome, Edge, and Samsung Internet have it everywhere; Firefox has it on desktop but not on Android; Safari has it from version 26.

There is no server-side fallback, deliberately. A fallback would mean uploading your video, which is the thing this site does not do.
